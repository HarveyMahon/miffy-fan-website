About Miffy

Miffy has been hopping through picture books for over seventy years. A few fun facts about how she came to be:

  1. A seaside bedtime story. Dutch artist Dick Bruna first drew her in 1955, after a holiday in Egmond aan Zee where he had been telling his young son stories about a little rabbit they'd seen hopping in the dunes.
  2. Her real name is nijntje. In Dutch she's called nijntje (roughly "nine-cha"), a shortening of konijntje, which means "little rabbit".
  3. She started out floppy. In the first book her ears flopped and she looked more like a fluffy toy. By 1963 Bruna had reworked her into the square-format books, with the upright ears and round face she's known for today.
  4. Always looking at you. Bruna drew Miffy facing straight out of the page, so young readers feel she is talking directly to them.
  5. A tiny paintbox. Bruna stuck to a handful of bold, flat colours (red, yellow, blue, green, plus a little brown and grey) with thick black outlines, and was famous for cutting away every line he didn't need.
  6. A mouth that's just a cross. Two short crossed lines do all the work, and readers read her mood into them.
  7. Twelve pages, four lines. Each classic Miffy book has twelve pictures, each paired with four lines of rhyming verse.
  8. Miffy was a boy at first. Early on the character wasn't clearly a girl. That changed around 1970, when Bruna drew her in a dress.
  9. A worldwide bunny. There are more than thirty Miffy books, translated into over fifty languages, with tens of millions of copies sold.
  10. Utrecht's favourite resident. Bruna's home city has a Miffy museum, a Miffy statue and even pedestrian traffic lights with Miffy as the walking figure. In later life Bruna joked that he'd gone from Miffy's father to her grandfather.
